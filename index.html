ABOUT

A software engineer who kept asking why the annotators disagreed

I'm a Software Engineer at Bdjobs.com Limited, where I work primarily with ASP.NET Core and Angular and contribute to an AI-based candidate-job matching system that compares applicant profiles with job requirements.

My research started with studying emotional language in cancer-related social media discussions. Working with thousands of posts and multi-annotator emotion labels made me interested in a problem that conventional evaluation often hides: people can legitimately interpret the same piece of language differently.

That question led me toward research on annotation disagreement, uncertainty, and human-AI interaction. My current work includes investigating whether model confidence affects how human annotators respond to suggested labels, evaluating uncertainty signals for ambiguity-aware dialogue, and studying robustness in low-resource Bangla NLP.

I studied Computer Science and Engineering at BRAC University. My broader research interests include NLP, machine learning, large language models, low-resource and code-mixed language understanding, uncertainty-aware learning, and human-centered AI.

RESEARCH FOCUS

Questions I keep returning to

I am particularly interested in NLP systems operating in situations where language is ambiguous, subjective, culturally dependent, or associated with sensitive real-world contexts.

ANNOTATION & HUMAN-AI INTERACTION

What happens when humans disagree with the model?

Studying annotation disagreement as meaningful information rather than simply noise, including whether model confidence influences human judgments on ambiguous items.

UNCERTAINTY IN LLMS

Can uncertainty signals reveal genuine ambiguity?

Evaluating semantic entropy and response-level signals as indicators of query ambiguity, and exploring feature-based approaches for ambiguity-aware dialogue.

LOW-RESOURCE NLP

How reliable are NLP methods for Bangla?

Studying low-resource and code-mixed Bengali text, including regional dialect identification, class imbalance, and NLP methods for language varieties with limited resources.

A Qualitative Study of the Mental Health Experiences of Cancer Patients with the Lowest Survival Rate and Their Caregivers

First author
IEEE ICCIT 2023 · DOI 10.1109/ICCIT60459.2023.10441282

Built a 10,087-post dataset from cancer-related online discussions, with 2,373 posts manually annotated for emotional valence and arousal. IN REVIEW

Current research

Class Imbalance in Regional Bangla Dialect Identification: A Benchmark Study with Interpretability Analysis

First author
Submitted to IEEE ICCIT 2026

Benchmarked class-weighting, oversampling, and SMOTE on BanglaDial using LinearSVC and Logistic Regression. The study evaluates whether commonly used imbalance techniques actually improve minority-class performance and examines dataset overlap and hierarchical classification as additional sources of evaluation bias.

Baseline macro-F1: 0.756 Hierarchical classifier: 0.797 macro-F1 Statistical testing: FDR-corrected

Adaptive Clarification: Evaluating LLM Uncertainty Signals for Ambiguity-Aware Dialogue

First author
Submitted to IEEE ICCIT 2026

Investigated whether semantic entropy can serve as a reliable proxy for query ambiguity in large language models. The study evaluates Qwen2.5-Instruct models at two scales and combines response-level uncertainty with query-level features for ambiguity classification and an ANSWER / HEDGE / CLARIFY response policy.

Semantic entropy AUC: 0.596 → 0.517 Fused features macro-F1: 0.450 → 0.571 Models: Qwen2.5 1.5B & 7B

AegisRL: An Adaptive Reinforcement Learning Framework for Alert Fatigue Mitigation in Security Operations Centers

Co-author
Submitted to IEEE ICCIT 2026

Contributed to a reinforcement learning framework for reducing alert fatigue in security operations centers through adaptive prioritization and intelligent alert routing.
